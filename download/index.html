How to install

Three steps to your Mac in tTerm

01

Open the file

Open the downloaded tTerm.dmg and drag tTerm to Applications.

02

Launch tTerm

The first time, macOS asks you to confirm, here is how. After that this Mac's terminals work right away, with no account and no setup.

03

Sign in when you like

Sign in opens your browser: confirm it, and your servers and notes appear in the window.

The first launch, just once

macOS 15 or later
Open tTerm and click Done. Then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security: at the bottom, next to the line about tTerm, click Open Anyway (the button shows for about an hour after your try) and confirm with your Mac's password.
macOS 11–14
In Finder, open Applications, Control-click tTerm, choose Open, then click Open again.

After that tTerm opens as usual and installs new versions by itself, and macOS does not ask about them.

Questions

How is it different from tTerm Web?

tTerm Web is a cloud terminal in your browser: the terminals of your servers and computers live in the cloud, and you open them from anywhere. tTerm for Mac is a local terminal on the Mac itself, with the whole cloud in its window. One account, the same servers and notes.

Is it free?

Yes. While tTerm is in beta it is free for everyone, the app too.

Do I need an account?

No. Without signing in, tTerm works with this Mac: shells, history and notes stay on it. An account is for the cloud: servers, notes on all your devices and this Mac from a browser.

Where are my commands kept?

This Mac's commands with their output stay on it for 30 days, in the app's folder that only you can read. Commands on servers are kept in the cloud, as in tTerm Web.

Is the app signed?

Yes, with tTerm's own certificate, and every update is signed with it too. There is no Apple Developer ID signature yet, so the first time macOS asks you to confirm, here is how. After that it does not ask again, updates included.

How does it update?

By itself. Every six hours tTerm checks for a new version, downloads it and installs it when nothing is in the way: when you quit, or when nothing is running in its terminals. In Settings → Updates you can have it ask you first.

How do I remove it?

Quit tTerm from the menu bar and drag it from Applications to the Trash. If you signed in, you can forget this Mac in Settings → Devices.

Is the code open?

Yes, under AGPL v3, like all of tTerm: github.com/tterm-net/tterm-app.
